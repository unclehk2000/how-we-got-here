# 2022 Bear Market Regime Test — Validation Against the 2023–2026 Findings

**The test:** 2022 was NQ's last major down year — **-30.8%** (16,383 → 11,340) and a US midterm election year. Every finding in v1–v5 was built on 2023–2026 data, a period that was on balance uptrending. Several conclusions were explicitly flagged as *"probably regime-dependent, needs a bear market to confirm."*

This document is that confirmation test. **237 clean sessions, all five session windows present.**

---

## HEADLINE

The framework held up better than expected. **Every setup flagged as structural survived. Every finding flagged as possibly regime-dependent did in fact change — in the exact direction predicted.**

That is close to the best possible outcome for a validation test: the things predicted to be robust were robust, and the things predicted to be fragile broke in the anticipated way rather than randomly.

---

# PART 1 — WHAT SURVIVED THE BEAR MARKET (Robust Across Regimes)

| Finding | 2023–2026 (bull) | **2022 (bear)** | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **London opening-block breakout** | 79.5% | **81.7%** (CI 73.7–87.7) | ✅ Held — slightly stronger |
| **Sweep-then-reverse (Asia)** | 31.6% | **27.8%** | ✅ Held |
| **Sweep-then-reverse (London)** | 26.0% | **27.8%** | ✅ Held |
| **Overnight range 50% retrace** | 75.8% | **74.7%** (CI 68.8–79.8) | ✅ Held — near identical |
| **10:00–10:30 reversal rate** | ~33% | **32.9%** (CI 27.2–39.1) | ✅ Held — near identical |
| **NY AM opening-block range forecast** | r=0.825 | **r=0.700** | ✅ Held — weaker but strong |
| NY PM opening-block breakout | 69.6% | **76.5%** | ✅ Held — stronger |
| Asia opening-block breakout | 68.8% | **68.2%** | ✅ Held — near identical |
| NY AM opening-block breakout | 60.8% | **60.8%** | ✅ Held — identical |
| First-10-min persistence | 55.8–68.6% | **62.9%** | ✅ Within range |
| Morning/day range correlation | r=0.605–0.783 | **r=0.611** | ✅ Within range |

### The three most important survivals

**1. London opening-block breakout: 81.7% in a -30.8% year.** This was the strongest setup in the entire project and it did not just survive a brutal bear market — it performed marginally better. Across five calendar years spanning a 30% decline and a multi-year rally, it sits in a tight 79–82% band. This is now the most thoroughly validated finding in the whole body of work.

**2. Sweep-then-reverse stayed broken in both regimes.** 27.8% in 2022, 26–32% in the bull years. The widely-taught "fade the liquidity sweep" pattern fails at roughly the same rate whether the market is rallying or collapsing. Continuation beats reversal 2–3x in *both* regimes. That's about as strong a refutation as this kind of data can produce.

**3. Overnight 50% retracement: 74.7% vs 75.8%.** Barely moved. Retracement-to-midpoint behaviour appears to be genuinely structural — a property of how the market fills value, not of trend direction.

# PART 2 — WHAT CHANGED (Regime-Dependent, As Predicted)

### ✅ Prediction confirmed: the OR breakout asymmetry was an uptrend artifact

| | Bull years | **2022 (bear)** |
|---|---|---|
| OR-**high** break holds | 82.0% | **84.6%** |
| OR-**low** break holds | **70.0%** | **81.9%** |

In the bull years, upside OR breaks were far more reliable than downside (82% vs 70%) — and I flagged that as almost certainly a trend artifact rather than structure.

**In 2022 the gap closed almost entirely** (84.6% vs 81.9%). Downside breakouts became nearly as reliable as upside ones once the market was actually falling.

**Practical implication:** the OR breakout setup is structurally sound in *both* directions. The 70% downside figure in the v3/v5 docs understates it — that number reflects trying to trade downside breaks in a rising market, not a flaw in the setup.

### ✅ Prediction confirmed: the weekly level asymmetry was an uptrend artifact

| | Bull years | **2022 (bear)** |
|---|---|---|
| Prior week's **high** revisited | 63.7% | **49.0%** |
| Prior week's **low** revisited | 38.0% | **53.1%** |

I flagged this one as "almost certainly a product of the uptrend; expect it to flatten or invert in a downtrend."

**It inverted.** In 2022 the prior week's *low* was revisited more often than the high — the exact mirror of the bull-market pattern.

**Practical implication:** do not use the 63.7%/38.0% figures as fixed. The correct reading is *"the level in the direction of the prevailing trend gets revisited more often."* Direction of the asymmetry follows the trend.

### ✅ Prediction confirmed: the NY AM high/low bias was an uptrend artifact

| | Bull years | **2022 (bear)** |
|---|---|---|
| NY AM contains day's **HIGH** | 44.4% | **46.8%** |
| NY AM contains day's **LOW** | 54.5% | **44.3%** |

The 10-point "NY AM more often holds the low" bias flattened completely in 2022. Same story: it was the uptrend, not structure.

# PART 3 — A GENUINELY NEW FINDING: SESSION CARRYOVER APPEARS IN TRENDING BEAR MARKETS

This is the one result that was **not** predicted, and it's the most interesting thing in this test.

In the bull years, every session-to-session directional carryover test was a clean coin flip. In 2022, two of them weren't:

| Test | Bull years | **2022** | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NY AM → NY PM** | 49.3% | **61.6%** (CI 55.3–67.6) | **+12.3 pts** |
| **London → NY AM** | 48.2% | **55.7%** (CI 49.3–61.9) | +7.5 pts |
| Asia → NY AM | 52.0% | 48.7% | no change |

**NY AM → NY PM at 61.6% is a real signal** — the confidence interval (55.3–67.6%) excludes 50% entirely. In 2022, if the morning session closed down, the afternoon session followed it down about 62% of the time.

**The interpretation, offered carefully:** 2022 was a sustained, persistent downtrend. In that kind of one-way market, intraday momentum carries across sessions in a way it simply does not during a choppy or rising market. This is a *trend-strength* effect, not a permanent property of the sessions.

**How to use this responsibly:** do not add "NY AM → NY PM carryover" to your standard playbook — it does not work in normal conditions (49.3% across four bull years). It is better understood as a **regime indicator**: if you notice session carryover starting to work, that itself is evidence you are in a strongly trending environment.

**Caveat:** this is one year, n=237. London → NY AM at 55.7% has a CI that just barely clears 50% and should be treated as suggestive only.

# PART 4 — SESSION PROFILES: 2022 vs BULL YEARS

| Session | Bull avg range | **2022 avg range** | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asia | 83 | 81 | −2% |
| London | 92 | **107** | **+16%** |
| NY AM | 188 | 195 | +4% |
| NY Lunch | 112 | 117 | +4% |
| NY PM | 138 | **169** | **+22%** |

**Two observations:**

1. **NY PM expanded 22% in the bear year** — afternoons got substantially more active. Consistent with the carryover finding above: 2022 afternoons were doing real directional work rather than drifting.

2. **NY AM barely moved (+4%)** despite a 30% annual decline. The morning session's volatility appears to be near a structural ceiling — it's already the most volatile window of the day in every regime.

**Directional balance in 2022:** every session's "% closes up" sat between 46% and 52%, even in a -30.8% year. The decline came from *magnitude* of down moves, not *frequency* — down days weren't much more common, they were bigger. That's a useful thing for a trader to internalise about bear markets.

# PART 5 — REVISED CONFIDENCE RANKINGS

With five years and two opposing regimes now tested:

### Tier A — Validated across bull and bear
1. **London opening-block breakout** — 79.5% / 81.7%. The single most robust setup found.
2. **Overnight range 50% retracement** — 75.8% / 74.7%
3. **Sweep continuation over reversal** — refuted the fade in both regimes
4. **10:00–10:30 reversal rate** — ~33% in every year tested, five years running
5. **NY AM opening-block range forecast** — r=0.700–0.825
6. **OR breakout** — now understood as strong in *both* directions (the downside weakness was a trend artifact)

### Tier B — Real but regime-sensitive
7. Weekly level revisits — **direction of asymmetry follows the trend**
8. NY AM high/low containment — same
9. NY PM / Asia opening-block breakouts — 68–77%, solid but more variable

### Tier C — Regime indicator, not a setup
10. Session direction carryover — coin flip normally; appears only in strongly trending markets. **Use it to identify the regime, not to trade.**

# WHAT THIS CHANGES IN THE BEGINNER'S GUIDE

Three corrections worth making:

1. **The OR downside break is stronger than stated.** The 70% figure should be presented as "70% in rising markets, ~82% in falling markets" rather than a flat number.

2. **The weekly level asymmetry needs a trend qualifier.** Currently written as "high 63.7% / low 38.0%" — should read "the level in the direction of the prevailing trend is revisited more often; expect roughly 64%/38% in an uptrend and the reverse in a downtrend."

3. **Add a regime-awareness note.** If session carryover starts working, that's a signal to expect trend persistence — and to expect the trend-sensitive stats above to shift accordingly.

Everything else in the guide stands as written.
