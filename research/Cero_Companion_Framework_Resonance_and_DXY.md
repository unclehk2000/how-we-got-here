## Part 1 — Where the Lecture Material Resonates With Our Own Established Findings

This isn't a case of adopting outside claims — every point below is something we found independently, through our own testing, that the lecture material also describes conceptually. Where the two agree, that's worth noting as real convergence, not borrowed authority.

### The midpoint of a range is mechanically real — the deepest resonance in this framework
The lecture material treats a range's midpoint ("consequent encroachment") as a genuine point of reaction, not just a visual reference. We didn't adopt that — we found it independently, six separate times, in six unrelated builds: the overnight range midpoint (67.4% honest retrace, Shikai), the RTH gap's own 50% octant (76.1%), the London Opening Range midpoint (Domain Expansion), Byakugan's 0.5 fib bias, the NDOG/NWOG midpoint fill mechanics, and Bankai's core target. Every model in this framework ended up with a midpoint-reaction component through independent testing. That consistency is the strongest single piece of convergence here.

### SMT divergence — not resonance, the same tool
The lecture material uses NQ-vs-ES divergence directly, including in an NFP context. Genjutsu Kai (Model 3) built exactly this and it produced the cleanest numbers anywhere in this framework — 73.7% to 97.9% retrace-through depending on session and signal type. This is the one place where we validated the exact tool with independent statistical rigor, not just a compatible idea.

### Liquidity persists well past its formation, and doesn't decay with reuse
The IPDA-lookback and NDOG/NWOG persistence work found that 62.8% of gaps get touched multiple separate times within 60 trading days — and critically, the reaction quality (slice-through rate, day-range on the touch) stayed flat regardless of whether it was the 1st or 4th visit. That's a sharper, quantified version of the "levels remain in play" idea the lecture material describes qualitatively.

### The four-phase structure of price delivery
Our Expansion/Retracement/Reversal/Consolidation classification and its transition matrix (Expansion→Retracement 42%, Consolidation→Consolidation 44%) were built from pure statistical classification of range-relative-to-trailing-average — not derived from the lecture framing — and it landed in nearly the same conceptual place the material describes.

### Multi-asset decoupling — we found a dated, real instance of it
The material describes markets moving in harmony during clear regimes and "decoupling" during uncertain ones. The NQ-DXY correlation work found exactly that with a real date attached: r ran −0.34 to −0.43 in 2023, 2024, and 2026, then collapsed to +0.084 (not significant) for the entirety of 2025. Not a confirmation of a vague idea — an empirical instance of the specific phenomenon.

### Specific internal timing windows, not just "the open is busiest"
London's real volatility peak sits at 03:00–03:30, not the 02:00 open. Bankai's sharpest entry window is 09:31–09:41, not the whole NY AM session. Both reject the naive "busiest right at the bell" assumption in favor of specific internal windows — matching the material's use of named macro windows through the session rather than just session opens.

### The one genuinely two-sided resonance: sweep behavior is regime-dependent, and both frameworks say so
Our general (non-NFP) sweep testing across NQ, gold, and silver found fading a sweep loses roughly 2:1 to continuation — one of the most thoroughly confirmed findings in this project. The NFP-specific material describes something that sounds like the opposite: an initial move designed to trap breakout-chasers before reversing. Our own NFP-specific data confirms *that* too — the two-stage reversal wins 65.8% of the time on NFP days specifically. These aren't contradictory: they describe two different regimes, and the lecture material's own repeated insistence that NFP behaves unlike an ordinary day is, in effect, the same claim we reached independently — this is a distinct regime with its own mechanics, not a scaled-up version of the everyday one.

### Where the specific numbers didn't hold up
Worth stating as plainly as the confirmations: the commonly-cited "~70% gap fill within 30 minutes" doesn't survive on NFP days (40.5%, and even the general non-NFP baseline only runs 52.6%, not 70%). "Wait 15 minutes for a clearer read" doesn't hold as a signal-quality claim — the post-wait window predicted the close slightly worse than the raw initial spike (36.8% vs 42.1%). And the minor-then-primary sequential liquidity narrative is real but a minority pattern (42.1% of days run both sides), not the dominant behavior.

## Part 2 — DXY on NFP: Does the Directly-Affected Instrument Help Read NQ?

NFP is a USD-denominated release. DXY is the instrument that reacts to it most directly — NQ's reaction is a second-order effect, filtered through risk sentiment and rate expectations. The question worth testing: does reading DXY's own NFP-day structure give you a better handle on NQ's day than reading NQ alone?

### DXY reacts even more violently to the release, relative to its own baseline, than NQ does

| | NFP avg range | Ratio vs. baseline |
|---|---|---|
| **DXY** | 0.526 | **139.4%** |
| NQ (from Model 6) | 324pts | 111.5% |

DXY's relative range expansion on NFP is substantially larger than NQ's. This makes sense given DXY is the direct instrument — NQ's reaction is one step removed, filtered through what the release implies for equities rather than reacting to the dollar move itself.

### DXY shows its own two-stage reversal — a real, if weaker, echo of NQ's

| | Result |
|---|---|
| DXY stage 2 (8:50–11:00) opposes stage 1 (8:30–8:50) | **57.9%** (CI 42.2–72.1%) |
| *(for reference: NQ's own figure)* | *65.8%* |

The disruption mechanic isn't unique to NQ — DXY shows the same lean, just less pronounced. This is consistent with the idea that NFP disrupts positioning broadly across instruments, not in one market specifically.

### The two instruments' reversal outcomes move together more often than not

| | Result |
|---|---|
| NQ's and DXY's two-stage reversal outcome match on the same day (both reverse, or both hold) | **60.5%** (CI 44.7–74.4%) |

When DXY's initial move reverses, NQ's tends to reverse too, and vice versa — a real, if not overwhelming, synchronization. This is the most actionable finding in this section: **watching whether DXY's own stage-1 move has reversed by the 8:50–11:00 window is a reasonable proxy for whether NQ's has too**, given they move together on this dimension roughly 3 times in 5.

### What did NOT hold up — the contrarian relationship doesn't sharpen on NFP days

This is the honest, important correction in this document.

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Same-day NQ/DXY close in opposite directions, NFP days only | 55.3% (CI 39.7–69.9%) |
| *(general all-day baseline, established elsewhere in this framework)* | *58.4%* |
| DXY's early (8:30–9:00) direction predicts NQ's eventual close via the contrarian relationship | **39.5%** (CI 25.6–55.3%) — **below chance** |

**NFP does not sharpen the basic NQ-DXY contrarian relationship — if anything, it's slightly weaker than the general baseline, and DXY's early read specifically fails as a contrarian predictor of NQ's eventual close.** The 39.5% figure means the *same*-direction outcome (DXY and NQ moving together, not opposite) actually happened more often across this specific window than the contrarian case. Given the wide confidence interval at this sample size, this shouldn't be read as "DXY becomes pro-cyclical on NFP days" — but it clearly rules out using DXY's early move as a reliable contrarian signal for NQ specifically on NFP mornings.

### A signal that could not be tested — sample too small to use

The DXY Opening-Range sweep-and-reverse signal (validated elsewhere in this framework at 70.7% on the general dataset) was tested on NFP days specifically and returned occurrences of only **n=3** and **n=4** — nowhere near enough to draw a conclusion despite one showing a 100% point estimate. That number is not reported as a finding here; it's flagged as data too thin to use. This signal may still work on NFP days, but this dataset can't confirm or deny it at usable confidence.

## What This Means in Practice

1. **Trust the midpoint mechanics and SMT divergence without hesitation** — both are independently validated at large scale elsewhere in this framework, and the lecture material's use of the same concepts is genuine confirmation, not coincidence.
2. **On NFP mornings specifically, DXY's own two-stage behavior is a reasonable cross-check on NQ's** — if DXY's initial move has clearly reversed by 8:50–11:00, that raises the likelihood NQ's has too (60.5% synchronization), and vice versa.
3. **Do not use DXY's early direction as a contrarian signal for NQ specifically on NFP days** — that relationship, which holds reasonably well on ordinary days, did not sharpen here and trended the wrong way in this sample.
4. **The DXY OR sweep-reversal signal remains unconfirmed for NFP days specifically** — too few occurrences in this dataset to use with any confidence; don't assume the general-case number (70.7%) transfers.
5. **DXY's disproportionately large reaction (139.4% of its own baseline, vs. NQ's 111.5%) confirms it as the more direct instrument** — consistent with treating NQ's NFP reaction as a second-order effect of what happens in the dollar first.

---
*All confidence intervals are 95% Wilson score intervals. DXY tested at 5-minute resolution across all 38 available NFP dates. Where sample size was insufficient for a reliable estimate, that is stated explicitly rather than reporting the raw percentage as a finding.*
