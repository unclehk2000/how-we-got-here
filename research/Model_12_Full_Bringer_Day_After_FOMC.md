# MODEL 12 — "FULL BRINGER"
### The Day After FOMC — An Empirical Analysis of Price Delivery
**40 next-day sessions, drawn from the same 43-meeting Vizard dataset (2018, 2022, 2023–2026). This model exists entirely because of an event it did not itself experience — its whole character is a residual consequence of the prior day, not something generated on its own terms.**

---

## Why "Full Bringer"

A Fullbringer's power isn't their own in the way a Shinigami's or a Hollow's is — it exists because of something that happened to someone else, before the Fullbringer's own story even started. That's the exact relationship this model has to Vizard. Nothing about the day after FOMC originates on that day. Every finding below is a downstream consequence of a decision that landed the previous afternoon, carried forward into a session that never itself touched the actual catalyst.

---

## Full Session Character — Mild, Uneven Elevation, Concentrated Overnight

| Session | Next-day avg. range | Baseline avg. range | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asia** | **117pts** | 100pts | **117.1%** |
| London | 152pts | 143pts | 106.3% |
| NY AM | 167pts | 168pts | 99.5% |
| NY Lunch | 134pts | 138pts | 96.7% |
| NY PM | 137pts | 130pts | 105.6% |

**The elevation is concentrated in the overnight session, not the U.S. day.** Asia — which for "the day after FOMC" actually means the evening immediately following the decision itself — runs a real 17% above baseline, the clearest single effect in this table. By the time NY AM and NY Lunch arrive, the session has normalized almost exactly to baseline (99.5%, 96.7%). This is a specific, useful correction to a natural assumption: **the day after FOMC is not uniformly louder than normal. Its own U.S. session is close to an ordinary day. What's actually elevated is the immediate overnight digestion of the event, hours before the next RTH session even opens.**

---

## Does the Next Day Sweep FOMC's Own High or Low?

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Sweeps FOMC day's high | 50.0% (CI 35.2–64.8%) |
| Sweeps FOMC day's low | 52.5% (CI 37.5–67.1%) |
| **Sweeps both** | **10.0% (CI 4.0–23.1%)** |

Each side individually is close to a coin flip, but sweeping *both* is rare — only 4 of 40 days. **The next day typically resolves in one direction, not two** — it's far more common for the session to commit to extending past one of FOMC's own extremes than to genuinely round-trip through both of them.

---

## FOMC Day's Own Range Is a Real, Strong Predictor of the Next Day's Range

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Correlation (FOMC day range → next day range) | **r = 0.520** |
| Next day's avg. range, after an above-median FOMC-day range | **356pts** |
| Next day's avg. range, after a below-median FOMC-day range | **179pts** |

**This is a genuinely strong relationship — nearly double the effect size found in most other cross-day tests built across this framework**, and the practical gap is enormous: a below-median FOMC day is followed by a next day running at essentially half the range of what follows an above-median one. If FOMC itself was violent, expect the next day to be too. If FOMC was contained, the next day tends to stay contained as well.

---

## Directional Continuation — Stronger at the Full-Day Level Than NY AM Alone

Vizard's own next-day work looked only at NY AM (9:30–11:00) and found a 55.8% continuation rate. Extending the same test to the full RTH session sharpens it:

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Next day's full RTH direction matches FOMC day's own direction | **60.0% (CI 44.6–73.7%)** |
| Next-day up-close rate (unconditional) | **40.0% (CI 26.3–55.4%)** |

**The continuation signature is real and slightly stronger across the whole session than it appeared when measured on NY AM alone.** The unconditional up-close rate — 40.0% — also extends Vizard's own documented down-lean (with its 2018-driven caveat) one further day: whatever pulled FOMC day itself down more often than not appears to still be exerting a mild pull the following day too, though this should be read with the same caution Vizard applies to its own directional finding, given the comparable small-sample construction here.

## Time and Price: Does the Next Day Front-Load Like NFP Does, or Pace Normally?

| Time | % of eventual full-day range already built |
|---|---|
| 10:00 | 42.7% |
| 11:00 | 66.1% |
| 12:00 | 74.3% |
| 13:00 | 77.6% |
| 14:00 | 86.7% |
| 15:00 | 93.3% |

**This is a genuine point of contrast with Cero.** NFP days were found to front-load their range construction well ahead of an ordinary day's own pace (45.9% by 10:00, 67.8% by 11:00 at baseline, against NFP's own 48.7% and 73.4%). The day after FOMC does not show the same front-loading — its completion pace at 10:00 and 11:00 (42.7%, 66.1%) sits close to, or fractionally behind, an ordinary day's own baseline rhythm. **The day after FOMC isn't rushing to deliver its range early. It's simply running somewhat wider than usual, at a normal pace, concentrated mainly in the overnight session that precedes it rather than in any specific rushed early-RTH window.**

## Carried Forward From Vizard, Restated Here for Completeness

Two findings originally documented in Vizard belong specifically to this model's subject and are restated here rather than left only in the parent document:

- **Next-day NY AM leans mildly toward following the statement over the press conference** (55.8% vs. 51.2%), a lean that holds even in the specific cases where the two faces disagreed with each other (54.5%).
- **The RTH gap 50% level taps less reliably the morning after FOMC** (55.8% vs. baseline 65.5%) — not because the session is quieter (it isn't, per the full session table above, once Asia's overnight elevation is accounted for), but because next-day NY AM carries real directional momentum forward rather than mean-reverting back through the gap.

## What This Means in Practice

1. **Don't expect a uniformly louder day.** The elevation lives in the overnight session immediately following FOMC, not in the next RTH session itself — NY AM and NY Lunch run close to an ordinary day's own pace.
2. **Use FOMC day's own range as your single best forecasting input for the next day.** r = 0.520 is a strong relationship by this framework's standards — a violent FOMC day is followed by a wider-than-normal next day roughly twice over; a contained one is followed by a contained one.
3. **Expect the next day to pick a side, not round-trip both of FOMC's extremes.** Both-swept happens only 10% of the time — plan around one-directional continuation past a single extreme, not a full sweep of the prior day's entire range.
4. **Lean toward continuation of FOMC's own direction, and don't expect it to front-load.** The 60.0% full-day continuation rate is real; the range builds at a normal, unhurried pace rather than rushing out early the way NFP does.
5. **This is a 40-event sample drawing on the same extended 2018/2022/2023–2026 dataset as Vizard.** Every figure here carries the same small-sample caution applied throughout that document — real, worth knowing, and not yet a settled law.

---
*All confidence intervals are 95% Wilson score intervals. Session and state definitions match Vizard and the rest of this framework exactly.*
