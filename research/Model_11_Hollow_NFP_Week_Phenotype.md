# MODEL 11 — "HOLLOW"
### The Phenotype of NFP Week — Price Structure, Monday Through Thursday
**38 NFP weeks tested, 2023–2026. This model covers the four days *leading up to* the release, not the release itself — Cero picks up from Friday morning. 140 individual NFP-week weekdays compared against 655 matched non-NFP-week baseline days.**

---

## Why "Hollow"

Everything else in this family — Cero, Gran Rey Cero, Pesquisa — describes an attack or the sensing that precedes one. A Hollow is what exists before any of that: the base creature, the raw shape and instinct, before it takes on a specific released form. That's exactly what this model is — the underlying phenotype of the week itself, before Friday's event gives it a specific direction to react to.

---

## Does Price Get Attracted to the Sunday Settlement (NWOG)?

Yes and no — and the "no" half is the more interesting finding.

**Yes: NFP weeks touch the NWOG more reliably than ordinary weeks.**

| | NFP weeks | Baseline weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Never touched Mon–Thu | 13.0% | **25.8%** |
| Touched at least once | **87.0%** | 74.2% |

**No: once touched, the week does not stay magnetized to it.** Distinct multi-touch weeks (2+ separate episodes) run nearly identical to baseline (17.4% vs. 18.3%) — no elevated persistence. And the distance from each day's close to the NWOG midpoint grows steadily, not shrinks, through the week:

| Day | Avg. distance from close to NWOG midpoint |
|---|---|
| Monday | **0.52%** |
| Tuesday | 1.31% |
| Wednesday | 1.65% |
| Thursday | **2.10%** |

**The honest picture: the NWOG gets engaged early — mostly Monday or Tuesday — and then the week moves on and steadily away from it, not back toward it.** Treat the NWOG as an early-week reference that resolves quickly, not a gravitational center the whole week orbits. This is consistent with what Cero already found about NWOG being resolved well before Friday in nearly every case.

---

## The Two States of NFP Week Delivery — Derived From the Data, Not Assumed

The expansion-reversal hypothesis was tested directly and rejected — but that's not the end of the story, just the wrong frame. Using directional efficiency (net move ÷ true range — how much of a day's actual travel became net progress rather than round-tripping) across every NFP week day and every baseline day, the distribution isn't evenly spread. It resolves into two natural regions:

- **Trend/Directional delivery** (efficiency ≥ 0.5 — at least half the day's range became net progress). The dominant mode in both NFP weeks and ordinary weeks.
- **Balanced/Two-sided delivery** (efficiency < 0.5 — the day traveled but gave most of it back). The minority mode, roughly a third of days either way.

**The complete null hypothesis, formally tested:** *NFP week's mixture of Trend vs. Balanced days equals baseline's mixture.*

| | Trend/Directional rate |
|---|---|
| NFP week (n=140) | 67.9% (CI 59.7–75.0%) |
| Baseline (n=655) | 63.8% (CI 60.1–67.4%) |

z = 0.907, p = 0.364. **Fail to reject the null.** Pooling all four NFP-week days together, there's no statistically distinguishable difference from an ordinary week's mix of trend versus balanced days — the same two underlying states, in the same rough proportions, just running somewhat larger in scale (consistent with the raw-magnitude finding above).

**Where the null breaks down: inside the week's own day-to-day structure.** Pooling Monday–Thursday together hides something real:

| Day | Trend/Directional rate |
|---|---|
| Monday | 58.1% |
| **Tuesday** | **80.6%** |
| **Wednesday** | **75.0%** |
| Thursday | 56.8% |

**Tuesday and Wednesday run meaningfully more trend-dominant than Monday and Thursday.** This directly explains the Wednesday finding above — it isn't only that Wednesday closes more bullish on average, it's that Wednesday (with Tuesday) is genuinely more likely to be a committed trend day in the first place, while Monday and Thursday lean close to a coin-flip between trending and giving it back. The week's character arcs from more two-sided (Monday) to more committed (Tuesday/Wednesday) to more two-sided again (Thursday) — real internal structure that a pooled weekly average erases.

## Where the Monday/Thursday Chop Actually Lives — Session-Level Breakdown

Isolating just the low-trending (efficiency < 0.5) Monday and Thursday days and breaking each one into its five sessions reveals two distinct, separate mechanisms — not one "choppy session," but two different things happening in two different places.

### NY AM is where the volume of chop physically concentrates

| Session | Avg. range | Own efficiency (net÷range) |
|---|---|---|
| Asia | 137 | 0.543 |
| London | 129 | 0.431 |
| **NY AM** | **161 — largest** | **0.368 — lowest** |
| NY Lunch | 133 | 0.475 |
| NY PM | 125 | 0.497 |

NY AM (9:30–11:00) is the widest session on these days by a real margin, but converts the least of that range into net progress — under 37% of it becomes actual displacement, the lowest of any session. This is the session doing the most work for the least directional payoff. **Median NY AM range on these days: 155pts. Median absolute net move: 65pts.**

### London is where the day's eventual direction actually gets defied

| Session | Moves opposite the day's eventual close direction |
|---|---|
| Asia | 37.9% |
| **London** | **53.6%** |
| NY AM | 34.5% |
| NY Lunch | 31.0% |
| NY PM | 41.4% |

London (2:00–9:30) is the only session more likely than not to point the *wrong* way relative to where the day eventually closes. Every other session leans — sometimes only modestly — toward already being aligned with the day's final direction. **Median London range on these days: 119pts. Median absolute net move: 42pts.**

**Read together: NY AM is where the chop's bulk lives; London is where the actual head-fake lives.** Different mechanisms, worth watching for separately rather than treating "Monday/Thursday chop" as one undifferentiated thing.

## RTH Octant Behavior on Monday and Thursday — No Single Reversal Point

Applying this framework's standard RTH gap octant ladder specifically to Monday/Thursday NFP-week days, tracking the farthest octant reached before the day turns back:

| Octant reached | Share of days |
|---|---|
| 0–3/8 (reverses early) | 21.9% combined |
| 4/8 | 8.8% |
| 5–6/8 | 8.8% combined |
| 7/8 | **13.2% — the closest thing to a secondary cluster** |
| **8/8 (full completion, no early reversal)** | **47.1% — the single most common outcome** |

**There is no single octant that functions as *the* Monday/Thursday reversal point.** The dominant outcome is that price runs the *full* distance to the far overnight extreme rather than reversing early — happening on nearly half of all days, more often than every partial-reversal outcome combined. Among the days that do stop short, there's no meaningful clustering at any early or mid-range level; the only real secondary grouping sits late, at 7/8, just short of full completion.

| | Full completion (8/8) |
|---|---|
| Monday | 38.7% |
| **Thursday** | **54.1%** |

Thursday completes the full octant ladder *more* often than Monday, not less.

**Reconciling this with the low-trending finding above — it's not a contradiction, it's two different measurements.** Directional efficiency measures where the day *closes* relative to its open. The octant ladder measures how far price *travels intraday* toward the overnight extreme. A day can fully complete the journey to the far extreme (8/8) and still close with poor net efficiency, by reversing hard on the way back after getting there. That's exactly what a large share of these days do: the chop isn't in whether price reaches its target — it usually does — it's in what happens on the way back afterward.

## RTH Gap 50% Fill — Which Session Delivers It, Day by Day

The framework's standard RTH gap 50% level (consequent encroachment) fills reliably across every day of NFP week — the real variation is in *which session* delivers it, and how much gets left unresolved.

| Day | NY AM | NY Lunch | NY PM | No fill during RTH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | **79.3%** | 6.9% | 6.9% | 6.9% |
| Tuesday | 71.4% | 7.1% | 3.6% | 17.9% |
| Wednesday | 66.7% | 5.6% | 5.6% | 22.2% |
| Thursday | 67.6% | 8.8% | 2.9% | 20.6% |

**NY AM dominates as the fill session on every day, but Monday is by far the most concentrated and reliable** — nearly 80% fill in NY AM alone, with only 6.9% of days leaving the level untouched by close. Wednesday and Thursday spread out more, with roughly 1 in 5 days leaving the 50% level unfilled entirely during RTH.

### Wednesday deep-dive

| | Result |
|---|---|
| 50% fill rate, full RTH | 77.8% |
| **Baseline (non-NFP) Wednesday, same measure** | **77.8% — an exact match** |
| Full gap fill rate (100%, back to prior close) | 69.4% |
| Median time to fill, when it fills | **5 minutes into RTH** |
| Fills within first 10 minutes | 60.7% |
| Fills within first 30 minutes | 75.0% |

**The overall fill rate isn't actually different from an ordinary Wednesday — what's different is where within the session it concentrates.** NFP-week Wednesday spreads the fill slightly more into "no fill" territory (22.2%) than Monday does, but the total probability of the 50% level filling at some point is identical to baseline. And when it does fill, it's fast — three out of four fills happen within the first half hour of the session, most within the first ten minutes. **If Wednesday's gap hasn't filled within the first 30 minutes, the base rate says it's more likely headed for "no fill" than a later-session resolution.**

## The Real Finding: A Bullish Build Through the Week, Then a Give-Back

This is the most significant pattern in the whole dataset, and it emerged from the day-by-day directional breakdown rather than anything hypothesized going in.

| Day | NFP-week avg net move | Baseline avg net move | Up-close rate (NFP / baseline) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | +46.7 | +33.6 | 61.3% / 59.8% |
| Tuesday | +32.7 | +15.4 | 63.9% / 58.3% |
| **Wednesday** | **+74.6** | **−4.4** | **61.1% / 50.0%** |
| **Thursday** | **−43.3** | −23.0 | 48.6% / 48.5% |

**Monday and Tuesday lean modestly more bullish than their baseline equivalents. Wednesday is where it becomes stark** — NFP-week Wednesdays average +74.6 points while ordinary Wednesdays sit essentially flat at −4.4, a genuine divergence in character, not just magnitude, and the up-close rate gap (61.1% vs. a coin-flip 50.0%) confirms it's a real directional lean, not a few large outliers. **Thursday gives a real portion of that back** — NFP-week Thursdays run more negative than baseline Thursdays (−43.3 vs. −23.0), though the up/down split stays nearly identical to baseline (48.6% vs. 48.5%), meaning the extra downside comes from larger down-moves on the days that do go down, not more frequent ones.

**This connects directly to Cero's own finding that NFP tends to move against the week's established direction (42.9% continuation).** Put together, the fuller picture is: the week builds a real bullish lean through Wednesday, Thursday already starts giving some of it back, and then NFP itself — per Cero — is more likely than not to continue that unwind rather than resume the Monday–Wednesday trend. Three separate pieces of this framework, built at different times for different purposes, now describe the same multi-day arc.

## The Settlement Window (16:14–17:00) — A Give-Back Signature and a Leading Indicator

Extending the analysis 46 minutes past RTH close, into the post-close settlement window, adds two genuinely new findings — one that sharpens the bullish-build-then-give-back story above, and one with real predictive value for the next day.

### Wednesday's own settlement window quietly gives back what RTH just built

| Day | Avg. settlement-window range | Avg. settlement-window net move |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 27.0pts | +1.9 |
| Tuesday | 43.1pts | −7.8 |
| **Wednesday** | **67.9pts — the week's peak** | **−21.1** |
| Thursday | 58.5pts | −5.9 |

Settlement-window volatility builds steadily through the week and peaks on Wednesday — the same day RTH itself runs its strongest bullish average (+74.6, from the finding above). **But the settlement window's own direction on Wednesday is meaningfully negative.** Wednesday builds its bullish case during RTH, then gives back a real piece of it in the 46 minutes right after the close — a miniature, same-day version of the broader Thursday unwind, compressed into Wednesday's own settlement period.

### The settlement window's size — not its direction — predicts the next day's range

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Correlation: settlement range → next day's RTH range | r = 0.209 |
| Next day's avg range, after an above-median settlement window | **371pts** |
| Next day's avg range, after a below-median settlement window | **237pts** |
| Settlement-window direction matching next day's own direction | 53.6% — a coin flip |

**Size carries real signal — a 56% difference in next-day range depending on which side of the median the settlement window falls on. Direction does not** — don't read the settlement window's net move as a forecast, only its size as one.

**One specific transition stands out well above the rest:**

| Transition | Correlation | Next-day range (above-median settle / below-median settle) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday → Tuesday | +0.24 | 364 / 226 |
| **Tuesday → Wednesday** | **+0.60 — more than double every other transition** | 360 / 223 |
| Wednesday → Thursday | +0.24 | 379 / 219 |
| Thursday → NFP Friday | +0.21 | 398 / 251 |

**Tuesday's post-close settlement window is a meaningfully sharper predictor of Wednesday's range than any other day-to-day transition in the week.** Given everything else already established about Wednesday being the week's real directional tell, this gives an earlier, concrete signal to watch for: Tuesday evening's settlement behavior specifically, not just Wednesday's own open. **Thursday's settlement window also carries forward into NFP Friday itself (r=0.21, 398 vs. 251pts)** — the first direct bridge in this framework between Hollow's week-long analysis and Cero's release-day numbers.

## Practical Takeaways

1. **Don't treat the NWOG as a week-long attractor.** It resolves early (usually Mon/Tue) and price moves steadily away from it afterward — use it as an early-week reference, not a target for Wednesday or Thursday.
2. **Don't expect NFP week's quiet days to be hiding unusual volatility.** The expand-then-settle-tight pattern is, if anything, less common in NFP weeks than in ordinary ones — the real structure is a Trend/Balanced state split, not hidden expansion.
3. **On Monday/Thursday specifically, separate the two chop mechanisms when reading tape.** NY AM (9:30–11:00) is where the bulk of the chop lives — widest range, lowest conversion to net progress (median range 155pts, only ~37% becomes displacement). London (2:00–9:30) is where the actual directional head-fake lives — the only session more likely than not to point opposite the day's eventual close (53.6%).
4. **Don't expect a clean early reversal point in the RTH octant ladder on Monday/Thursday.** Price most often runs the *full* distance to the far overnight extreme (47.1% of days) rather than reversing early — the chop on these days tends to happen *after* reaching the target, not before.
5. **Monday is the most reliable day for an early, NY-AM-concentrated 50% gap fill (79.3%, only 6.9% no-fill).** Wednesday and Thursday spread out more, with roughly 1 in 5 days leaving the level unfilled — and on Wednesday specifically, if it hasn't filled within 30 minutes, "no fill" becomes the more likely outcome.
6. **Watch Wednesday's own settlement window (16:14–17:00), not just its RTH session.** It peaks in size for the week and runs meaningfully negative even as RTH itself closes strongly bullish — a same-day preview of the broader Thursday give-back.
7. **The settlement window's size is a real leading indicator for the next day's range (371pts vs. 237pts, above- vs. below-median) — its direction is not.** Don't treat a big settlement-window move as a directional forecast.
8. **Tuesday's settlement window specifically deserves extra attention** — it predicts Wednesday's range more than twice as strongly (r=0.60) as any other day-to-day transition in the week.
9. **Tuesday's overnight sessions (Asia/London) run meaningfully wider than normal** — a real, specific window worth extra attention, and one that echoes the same pattern found around CPI.
10. **Wednesday is the week's real directional tell.** A materially more bullish signature than an ordinary Wednesday shows up here consistently, and Wednesday (with Tuesday) is genuinely more likely to be a committed trend day, not just a bigger one — worth treating as the point where the week's positioning becomes visible.
11. **Thursday's mild quieting and partial give-back sets up Friday**, and its own settlement window carries real predictive signal into NFP Friday's eventual range (r=0.21). Combined with Cero's finding that NFP tends to reverse the week's direction, don't assume Wednesday's strength survives into the release.
12. **This is a 38-week sample.** Every figure here is a real, worth-knowing lean — hold it with the same caution applied throughout this framework, not as a settled law.
