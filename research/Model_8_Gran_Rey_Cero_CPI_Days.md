# MODEL 8 — "GRAN REY CERO"
### CPI Day Price Delivery — A Walk-In Framework
**Built from Model 7's tested findings (42 CPI releases, Jan 2023–Aug 2026) and the concepts recurring across the CPI-specific lecture material. Where the two agree, that's stated. Where the lecture's folklore and the measured data pull apart, that's stated too — the framework is built to hold both honestly rather than pick one.**

---

## Why "Gran Rey Cero"

A Gran Rey Cero is what a Cero becomes in the hands of someone the lecture material treats as more dangerous, more unpredictable, more worth fearing than the original. That's exactly how CPI gets talked about relative to NFP throughout the source material — "dark waters," a boulder dropped into a still pond, a day where "you can't trust even my own concepts," a report the lecturer has "been burned by many times."

**But Model 7's own numbers tell a different story about magnitude.** CPI's average displacement runs *smaller* than an ordinary day, not larger. Its NY AM volatility bump is modest (106.5% of baseline) next to NFP's real spike (120%). Its disruption mechanic — the thing that makes NFP genuinely dangerous to be positioned against — doesn't show up in the data at all.

**The name is earned by CPI's reputation and its difficulty to read, not by its size.** That distinction is the first thing this framework asks you to hold onto: CPI isn't a bigger hit than NFP, it's a different, more spread-out, easier-to-misjudge one — and misjudging it by assuming it behaves like NFP is exactly the mistake this framework exists to prevent.

---

## Stage 0 — Ground Rules Before You Do Anything Else

- **This is a study day before it's a trading day** — more emphatically so than NFP, if the lecture material's own language is any guide. Reduce size well below what you'd run on an ordinary session, or work in simulation entirely until your own back-tested experience with this specific event justifies more.
- **No fundamental analysis.** The headline number, whether it beat or missed consensus, is irrelevant to this system. Only price's reaction carries information.
- **Every number below comes from a 42-event sample.** Treat it as a real, directional lean — never a settled law.

---

## Stage 1 — Build the Dealing Range Before 8:30

Before the release, mark three things:

1. **The pre-release range** — the high and low formed in roughly the hour before 8:30. This is the structure the release will test.
2. **Yesterday's RTH close** — the anchor for the opening-range-gap concept used throughout Stage 4.
3. **Any still-open NWOG or NDOG** — check, but don't expect to find one (see Stage 5).

Grade the liquidity around that range the same way as Cero: **minor** (the immediate pre-release swing high/low) vs. **primary** (the prior day's or week's relative-equal levels). This costs nothing to do and gives you the reference structure the rest of the framework hangs on.

---

## Stage 2 — The Boulder in the Pond: Absolute No-Trade Window

**8:30–8:45 is observation only.** The lecture material is unusually blunt about this for CPI specifically — liquidity providers pull access during this window, fills are unreliable even in simulation, and the first move is functionally untradeable regardless of skill.

This isn't just caution talking. Tested directly:

| | Result |
|---|---|
| First liquidity side swept predicts the day's larger move runs the *opposite* way | 42.9% — **not confirmed, and if anything leans the other way** |

**The specific claim that the first sweep is a decoy pointing you toward the real move does not hold.** Note which side gets hit first if you want, but don't trade off it as a contrarian signal. Its only real value here is context for Stage 3.

---

## Stage 3 — Let the Ripples Settle (from ~8:45)

Once the initial shock has passed, the lecture material's own framing is that "opportunities" open up — not from chasing the release candle, but from genuine structure: fair value gaps, order blocks, breaker blocks, each validated by how price reacts at its consequent encroachment (the 50% level).

This is also where cross-market confirmation earns its place — **use it here, not earlier:**

- NQ vs. ES divergence (SMT), same mechanic as Genjutsu Kai
- Dow vs. NASDAQ divergence
- EUR/GBP or DXY divergence against equities

None of these are meant to front-run the release. They're a confirmation layer for a directional read that's already starting to form from price structure itself.

---

## Stage 4 — The Opening-Range Gap: A Slow-Burn Target, Not a Fast One

The lecture material treats the gap between yesterday's close and today's open as something price gravitates back toward early. Tested precisely:

| | CPI | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Gap 50%-fill within the first 30 minutes (9:30–10:00) | **43.6%** | 52.5% |
| Full gap fill by end of day | 53.8% | 58.1% |

**The fast version of this idea doesn't hold — CPI fills the gap more slowly than an ordinary day, not faster.** But the full-day figure sits much closer to baseline. **Translation: keep the gap and its midpoint on your reference sheet, but treat them as a target for later in the session, not the opening half hour.**

---

## Stage 5 — NWOG/NDOG: Background Reference, Not a Live Target

| | Result |
|---|---|
| NWOG still unfilled the day before CPI | 16.0% |

**84% of the time, the week's opening gap is already resolved before CPI even happens.** Same pattern found in Cero for NFP weeks. Don't hunt for NWOG as something CPI is going to deliver into — it's almost always old business by release day. On the rare week it's still open, treat it as background context only; the sample of genuinely live cases here is too thin to build real conviction on.

---

## Stage 6 — Where the Volatility Actually Lives

The instinct, carried over from NFP, is to expect one concentrated NY AM spike. **CPI's own session map looks different:**

| Session | vs. baseline |
|---|---|
| Asia | **124.5% — wider**, not quieter |
| London | 88.8% — quiet |
| NY AM (the release itself) | **106.5% — only modestly wider** |
| **NY Lunch** | **113.3% — genuinely elevated** |
| NY PM | 98.7% — normal |

**Don't front-load all your attention onto the NY AM window expecting an NFP-style spike.** Real, tradeable movement on CPI days is more evenly spread, with the NY Lunch macro window (11:30–1:30) carrying more weight than it does around NFP. Budget attention accordingly.

---

## Stage 7 — Reading Distribution Once the Day Develops

The lecture material's qualitative framework for the later session: watch for a sequence of short-term highs (or lows) getting taken out one after another. Each one is treated as smart money offsetting a position into the liquidity that new short-term extreme provides, not as fresh conviction pushing the market further. This is a discretionary structural read — a form of PD array — not something tested directly from OHLC data in this framework, the same convention applied to Cero's balance-price-range concept. Apply your own confirmed version of this if you have one; treat it as a later-session lens, not an entry trigger on its own.

---

## Stage 8 — The Directional Priors the Data Actually Supports

| | CPI | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Up-close frequency | **61.9%** | 55.2% |
| Average absolute displacement | **119.6 (smaller)** | 152.3 |

**A frequency edge, not a magnitude edge.** Lean slightly bullish in bias, but don't size for an outsized move — the data says the opposite of what you'd expect.

| | CPI | *(NFP, for contrast)* |
|---|---|---|
| Week's direction matches CPI's own direction | 52.5% — essentially neutral | *42.9% — a real opposing lean* |
| Next day continues CPI's own direction | **56.1% — leans toward continuation** | *36.8% — leans strongly toward reversal* |

**Do not import Cero's "expect disruption" assumption onto CPI.** The mechanism that makes NFP genuinely dangerous to hold a position against simply isn't present here — if anything, the lean runs the other way.

---

## Stage 9 — DXY as a Loose Cross-Check

| | CPI | *(NFP)* |
|---|---|---|
| DXY range vs. its own baseline | 116.6% | *139.4%* |
| Same-day NQ/DXY contrarian rate | 57.1% | *55.3%* |

DXY still reacts more than usual, just less violently than around NFP. Use its own developing structure as a loose secondary check the way it's used in Cero's companion — don't expect the contrarian relationship itself to sharpen meaningfully around CPI specifically.

---

## The Walk-In Checklist

**Before 8:30**
1. Mark the pre-release dealing range, yesterday's RTH close, and check for any still-open NWOG/NDOG.
2. Grade nearby liquidity as minor (near) or primary (far).

**8:30–8:45**
3. Observe only. Note which side gets swept first — don't trade off it.

**8:45 onward**
4. Look for genuine structure — fair value gaps, order blocks — confirmed by reaction at consequent encroachment.
5. Layer SMT divergence (NQ/ES, Dow/NASDAQ, DXY) once a read is forming, not before.
6. Treat yesterday's close / the opening-range gap as a later-session target, not an opening-half-hour one.

**Through the session**
7. Expect real volatility to persist into NY Lunch (11:30–1:30), not just NY AM — don't front-load attention onto the open alone.
8. Carry a mild up-close bias as a loose prior, sized for a smaller-than-average move.
9. Don't expect CPI to disrupt the week's trend or reverse into the next session — if anything, lean toward mild continuation.

**Throughout**
10. Reduce size and conviction across the board. This is a 42-event sample describing one of the hardest release types to read, by the lecture material's own repeated admission — treat that warning as current, not historical.
