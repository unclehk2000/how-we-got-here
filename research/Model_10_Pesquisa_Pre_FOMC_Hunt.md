# MODEL 10 — "PESQUISA"
### The Pre-FOMC Liquidity Hunt — A Seek-and-Destroy Framework
**Built from lecture material spanning 2022–2026 that names this pattern directly, paired with Model 9's tested findings on FOMC's actual release-day mechanics. Where the two agree, that's stated. Where the lecture's confidence outruns what the data supports, that's stated with equal clarity.**

---

## Why "Pesquisa"

Cero and Gran Rey Cero are the strike — the concentrated blast a hollow releases once it's closed on its target. But a hollow doesn't find its prey by accident. It uses Pesquisa first — a sensing ability that locates and tracks a target's spiritual pressure across distance, before the attack ever happens.

That's exactly the phase this model covers. The lecture material describes something that happens **before** FOMC's 2:00 PM release, not during it: price chopping between two known pools of resting liquidity in the hours and sessions leading up to the decision, in a way that looks aimless but is actually mapping the battlefield — finding out who's resting stops where, before the real move ever begins. One of the source lectures names this pattern outright as a hunt. Pesquisa is the sensing that precedes the Cero, not a replacement for it — this model is meant to sit alongside Model 9 and Gran Rey Cero, not instead of them.

---

## What the Lecture Material Actually Describes

Stripped of its own vivid language and restated plainly, the pattern has four recurring components:

**1. Price runs between two mapped pools, not toward a clean directional target.** In the run-up to FOMC (and comparably, the opening day of a multi-day event like the Jackson Hole Symposium), price is described as sweeping one side's stops, reversing, running the other side's stops, and repeating — genuinely resembling noise to an untrained eye, but organized around specific, identifiable levels rather than random.

**2. A distinct "corking" sub-pattern.** Price arrives near a key level and, instead of releasing cleanly through it, builds a tight, sloppy consolidation right at that level — repeatedly probing without committing. The lecture material compares this directly to pressure building under a cork before it pops. This is described as a slow, nerve-testing process, not a fast one — and it's flagged as a signature of the level actually mattering, not of the market being directionless.

**3. Clean, untested levels are treated as bait, not safety.** A recent high or low that hasn't been touched or tested — described repeatedly as "too smooth" — is treated with active suspicion rather than trust. The reasoning given is direct: a level that looks safe to rest a stop behind is exactly the level the move is more likely to be aimed at, precisely because it looks safe.

**4. "Priming" — deliberately building false confidence in a level before invalidating it.** The material describes a level being tested and holding a few times specifically so that participants come to trust it as real support or resistance — only for that trust to be the setup for the level's eventual, often violent, failure.

**One more structural detail is specific to FOMC and worth carrying forward on its own: because the meeting itself runs two days, the first day's high and low (referred to in the material as "Tuesday's high" for a typical Tuesday–Wednesday meeting) function as a real, FOMC-specific reference level that the second day's price action frequently seeks out before the decision ever lands.**

## Where This Confirms Model 9 — and Where It Doesn't

This is the part worth being precise about, because the lecture material states its directional claims with total confidence, and not all of them survive contact with the data already gathered.

**Confirmed:** Model 9 found NY AM ahead of FOMC running at just 67.2% of its own baseline — genuinely, measurably quieter than normal. That's the exact condition the seek-and-destroy framing describes: a suppressed, coiled state before the release, not ordinary trading. The qualitative description and the quantitative finding agree here.

**Not confirmed — and worth saying plainly:** The lecture material describes FOMC's two-part structure (2:00 statement, 2:30 press conference) with real conviction — the first move is "not the real move," a knee-jerk; the second is "the real move," described in one source as a tsunami. Tested directly in Model 9, the press-conference window opposed the statement window's direction only **51.2%** of the time — a coin flip, not a reversal pattern. **The market's tendency to build tension and release through a level (corking, priming) is a real, useful pattern-reading skill. Whether that release goes the same direction as the first move or the opposite direction is not something this data can predict — treat the two as genuinely independent, not as a fade setup.**

**Also not confirmed:** The material's repeated framing — that the event exists to disrupt whoever's currently profitable, running toward whichever side would cause "maximum damage" — is the same disruption mechanic Cero found real evidence for around NFP. Model 9 tested this specifically for FOMC and found the week's direction matching FOMC's own direction at **46.5%**, essentially neutral. **Don't import the "attack the profitable side" assumption as a directional edge — it's a vivid way of describing the market's behavior, not something the numbers back up as a reliable prediction.**

This distinction matters throughout the framework below: **use Pesquisa to read structure and location. Don't use it to predict direction.**

## The Walk-In Framework

### Stage 1 — Map the Hunting Ground (before the pre-release quiet sets in)
Mark, in order of priority:
- The prior trading day's high and low
- **The FOMC meeting's own Day 1 high and low** — this is the FOMC-specific level the material returns to repeatedly, and it isn't something Cero or Gran Rey Cero track
- The current week's relative-equal highs and lows
- Any fair value gaps sitting nearby, graded into their low / lower-quadrant / consequent-encroachment (midpoint) / upper-quadrant / high structure

### Stage 2 — Flag the Suspiciously Clean Levels
Before the session develops, note which of the levels from Stage 1 look untested and smooth. Per the lecture material's own heuristic, **treat those as more likely targets, not less** — don't rest directional conviction on a level just because it looks like it should hold.

### Stage 3 — Read the Pre-Release Chop as a Hunt, Not Noise
Once the session is underway, expect price to move between the Stage 1 levels in a way that looks aimless. This is consistent with Model 9's own finding that NY AM ahead of FOMC runs at only 67.2% of its normal range — genuinely suppressed, coiled activity, not a session to force trades into. Observe which pools get swept and which don't; don't trade the chop itself.

### Stage 4 — Watch Specifically for Corking
If price arrives at one of the mapped levels and starts building a tight, repetitive consolidation right against it rather than releasing cleanly, treat that as the specific signature described in the source material — rising pressure at a level that matters, not a sign the level is safe. This is a slow pattern; don't rush its resolution.

### Stage 5 — Track Unfinished Business
Any pool or gap that gets approached during the pre-release chop but not fully swept stays live. The material treats these as the most likely targets once the actual release provides the energy to complete them — carry this list into the 2:00 PM window rather than discarding it once the pre-release session ends.

### Stage 6 — At 2:00, Switch From Hunting to Observing
This is where Pesquisa hands off to Gran Rey Cero's own Stage 2. The lecture material's own stated rule is not to act until roughly 2:45 — and Model 9's session-character data backs the underlying instinct even if not the specific reversal claim: this is real, measured, extreme volatility (254.8% and 282.8% of baseline across the two release windows). **Use the first move to see which of your Stage 1/Stage 5 levels gets engaged — not to predict which way the second move will go.**

### Stage 7 — Layer the Measured Priors Underneath Everything Above
Once structure and location are read using Stages 1–6, weight the actual decision using what Model 9 found, not the lecture's directional folklore:
- A mild lean toward a down-close day (41.9% up vs. 53.9% baseline), with larger-than-normal displacement
- No reliable statement-vs-press-conference reversal (51.2%) — treat both windows independently
- Gap-fill runs *above* baseline (73.0% vs. 57.7%) — don't expect the morning's gap to stay open
- No disruption mechanic — the week's direction and the following session both run close to neutral or lean toward continuation
- DXY as the strongest secondary read of the three scheduled events in this framework (192.5% of its own baseline, 63.0% contrarian rate with NQ)

## The Condensed Checklist

1. Before the session: mark prior-day high/low, **the meeting's own Day 1 high/low**, weekly relative-equal levels, and grade nearby gaps into quadrants.
2. Flag clean, untested levels as suspect — not safe.
3. Expect the pre-release session to chop between these pools; this is suppressed, coiled activity (measured at 67.2% of normal NY AM range), not a trading opportunity in itself.
4. Watch for corking — tight consolidation building at a level without releasing — as a sign of rising pressure, not safety.
5. Track which pools get left unfinished; carry them forward as live targets into the release.
6. At 2:00, observe only. Use the first move to see which levels get engaged — don't use it to predict the second move's direction; the data shows no reversal tendency (51.2%).
7. Apply Model 9's measured priors — mild down-lean, larger displacement, elevated gap-fill, no disruption mechanic, DXY as the sharpest secondary confirmation — as the actual quantitative backbone underneath this structural read.
8. Treat all of this, per the source material's own repeated and explicit instruction, as a study exercise first. Reduce size well below normal or work in simulation until your own experience with this specific pattern justifies more.
